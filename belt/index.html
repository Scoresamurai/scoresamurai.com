The five belts

What each belt means, and what stops working there

A move is closed when it stops costing you points. Close a belt's moves and the next belt is earned. A belt shows what is closed. It never predicts a score.

  1. White

    Under 1100
    Your belt

    The ground floor. The points here go to setup, not to hard content: reading what the question asks, saying what each number stands for, and tracking the right line.

    What stops working at this belt

    • Reading the answer choices before you know what the blank needs.
    • Starting the algebra before you've said what each number stands for.
    • Reading a number from the wrong row of a table.
    • Picking a sentence that's fine but written for the wrong reader or the wrong goal.

  2. Green

    1100 to 1250
    Your belt

    The trap belt. You know the content. The points go to answers that are built to look right.

    What stops working at this belt

    • Using the right method and stopping one step short of what was asked.
    • Picking a true answer to a question nobody asked.
    • Trusting the first meaning of a word when the test wants another one.
    • Joining two full sentences with a comma alone.
    • Judging a shaded graph by eye instead of testing a point.

  3. Blue

    1250 to 1350
    Your belt

    The clock belt. You can solve almost everything. The points go to time, spent the long way when a faster read was sitting right there.

    What stops working at this belt

    • Solving a whole system when the slopes already gave the answer.
    • Working a question out by hand when Desmos would settle it in seconds.
    • Solving all the way through when an estimate would do.
    • Letting an answer the passage ruled out come back in new words.
    • Keeping a verb that sounds fine but breaks the time frame.

  4. Brown

    1350 to 1450
    Your belt

    The hard-module belt. These are the questions in the harder second module, where one small slip turns a right method into a wrong choice.

    What stops working at this belt

    • Treating a squared change as if it grew in a straight line.
    • Reading more into a chart than it shows.
    • Letting one extreme value fool you about an average.
    • Treating a trend line as if every point sat on it.
    • Putting commas around words the sentence can't do without.
    • Picking a choice that's only half right.

  5. Black

    1450 and up
    Your belt

    The rare-question belt. Each of these shows up once or twice a test. At this score, they're what is left.

    What stops working at this belt

    • Adding up growth rates that actually build on each other.
    • Shifting a graph the wrong way.
    • Taking the plain average of two averages when one group is bigger.
    • Picking a claim the text never makes.
