The un-school test

Why the SAT matters more now.

MIT, Dartmouth, Harvard and Stanford require test scores again. Each said scores help predict how students do once they arrive.

The way Sensei Jay reads it, that's only half the story. AI now sits inside schoolwork. Grades have crept up for years, and an A means something different at every school. The SAT is the one test every applicant takes under the same rules.

The Dojo

A Sensei, a Roadmap, and a belt for every set of moves you close.

Sensei Jay saw the SAT for what it is. It is a knowledge test, but also a game of moves. The same patterns come back every year, the way a martial art repeats its forms.

So he built Score Samurai to turn students into SAT Samurai Warriors. He became the Sensei and opened the Dojo. He hands out belts as each student closes the moves that used to cost them points. Each belt means a set of the test's moves no longer works on them.

  • White

    Held from the Diagnostic. Every student starts here.

  • Green

    Closes when the first set of moves on the Roadmap stops costing points across two practice tests.

  • Blue

    Closes when the timed moves are closed and Desmos is a habit.

  • Brown

    Closes when the hard-module moves in one section stop costing points.

  • Black

    Closes when the hard-module moves in both sections stop costing points.

Nobody hands you Green. You take it.

Five principles

The Samurai Way.

  1. The test has moves. The SAT repeats the same moves every year. We teach the moves, not the subjects.

  2. Your misses are the curriculum. The Roadmap is built from them, in the order they cost points. No syllabus, no two the same.

  3. Points come back in order. The next hundred live in the most frequent misses at your score, never in the hardest topic.

  4. Never react. Read. The test is built to make a student rush. A Warrior reads the tell first.

  5. Earn the belt. Each one means a set of the test's moves no longer works on you.

Most prep teaches the subjects. The Dojo teaches the test's moves.

Built by Sensei Jay. Taught by Sensei Jay. Score Samurai takes on a limited number of students because every session is taught by him.

In the Dojo

How Sensei Jay teaches.

I teach one move at a time, and I never rush it. I show the move once. Then the student does the solving while I watch for the habit that cost the points.

Every move gets a name, so it can be spotted before it fires. We finish when the student can say the rule in their own words. That is the only version I trust to hold up on test day.

Sensei Jay, The SAT Sensei
The method

The Digital SAT is a pattern recognition game, not purely an intelligence test.

Built from 1,168 real SAT questions, catalogued one at a time.

The Dojo Roadmap is the plan. It is built from the student's own misses, not from a syllabus. It lists the moves to close, in the order they cost points at that score. It is rewritten as belts are earned. No two Roadmaps are the same.

You already know the material. You are losing points to habits that have names.

The same moves, every test. The wrong answers are built from the same handful of habits, year after year. A procedure stopped one step early. A first meaning trusted too fast. A true sentence that answers a question nobody asked. Once a student can name the move, it stops working.

The Desmos work. Desmos is built into the Math section. Half of the timing problem is knowing which questions to send to it. The rest is knowing which to work by hand. We decide that question by question, until the choice is a reflex.

Weekly tracking. Speed and accuracy are tracked every week, by section, and reported to the parent in the written note. Nobody has to ask how it is going.

Why students get stuck. No two students are stuck for the same reason. One loses points to the clock. Another picks the answer that is true and still wrong. A third knows the math cold and freezes when a question looks unfamiliar.

The way I see it, AI can answer the question. It cannot see why this student keeps missing it. That is the difference between an app and a Sensei.

Sensei Jay, The SAT Sensei, founder of Score Samurai

More questions

Scores, colleges and dates.

When do SAT scores come back?

About two weeks after the test.

For the 2026-27 dates, scores release 13 days after test day. The June sitting takes 16.

Source: College Board score release dates, satsuite.collegeboard.org, read October 9 2026.

Pick the sitting you want to be your last, then count back.

What is a good SAT score?

A good SAT score is one that sits inside the middle 50 percent at the schools on your list.

Nationally, a 1200 is the 81st percentile and a 1400 the 97th. Source: College Board, research.collegeboard.org, read October 12 2026. See where any score from 1000 to 1500 stands.

Good is whatever gets you into the schools on your list. For one student that's a 1200, and for another it's a 1450. The Diagnostic starts with your list and works back to the score each school expects. Then it finds the moves that get you there.

Do colleges require the SAT?

Yes. Ten of the most selective schools require the SAT or ACT now, and more join every year.

Required now: Brown (from the 2024-25 cycle), Caltech (fall 2027 applicants) and Cornell (fall 2026 entry). Also Dartmouth and Harvard (both Class of 2029 on), and Johns Hopkins (fall 2026 entry). Plus MIT, Penn (fall 2026 applicants), Stanford (fall 2025 applicants on) and Yale.

Required later: Columbia and Princeton from fall 2028 entry, Notre Dame from fall 2028 entry, Vanderbilt from fall 2029 entry. Each stays test-optional until then. Every date is from the school's own admissions page, read October 9 2026.

The SAT is the one test every applicant takes under the same rules.

Many other schools stay test-optional. A strong score still helps there, and policies change, so check each school's own page.
