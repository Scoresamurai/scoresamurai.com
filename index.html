The Belt Ladder

Five belts. Each one means a set of traps stops working.

The Diagnostic places you at the belt your score has already earned. Each belt has its own named patterns. A pattern is closed once it stops costing points on two practice tests in a row. Close the whole set and Sensei Jay logs the next belt on your Roadmap.

  1. White

    The first belt on the ladder.

  2. Green

    Closes when the first set of moves on the Roadmap stops costing points across two practice tests.

  3. Blue

    Closes when the timed moves are closed and Desmos is a habit.

  4. Brown

    Closes when the hard-module moves in one section stop costing points.

  5. Black

    Closes when the hard-module moves in both sections stop costing points.

A belt shows what is closed. It never predicts a score.

Questions

Frequently asked.

What does it cost?

The plan sets the number of sessions, and the free Diagnostic sets the plan. The price comes in writing with the Diagnostic, before you decide anything.

Do you guarantee a score?

No. Guarantees in test prep come with pages of conditions, and a score depends on the student doing the work between sessions. What is promised is this: a written Diagnostic before you pay, the same teacher in every session, and a written update every week so you can see for yourself whether the traps are closing.

We already use Khan Academy and an AI tutor. Why this?

Keep them. They are the gym. AI can answer the question. It cannot see why this student keeps missing it, and it will not notice when the same trap fires three weeks in a row. Sensei Jay reads the misses, decides what to fix first, and holds the student to it.

Is there a call?

Only if you want one. You can book a free 15-minute Zoom with Sensei Jay, a parent on, when you submit the form or after the written Diagnostic arrives. He walks through the Diagnostic and the plan. No pitch deck. It is optional; the written Diagnostic has everything a family needs to decide.

What if the student is not motivated?

Most are not, at the start. The sessions are built for that: one trap at a time, a question they win alone at the end of every session, and a weekly update that makes progress visible. Motivation tends to follow the first closed trap.

Why fifty minutes?

In Sensei Jay's experience, hard focus fades after about fifty minutes. So each session takes a few moves from the Roadmap and teaches them, drills them, then ends on a fresh question the student has to win alone. The work between sessions is where the moves set.

When should we start?

Pick the sitting you want to be your last, then count back. Start with enough runway and the plan has a backup date built in. The free Diagnostic sets the date.

What happens to our information?

It is used to prepare the Diagnostic and for nothing else. No list, no resale. Privacy
